# Adaptive rate control, rate-step stage (acceptance A4): does ADAPTIVE beat a fixed rate when the link changes? (2026-11-23)

**An emulation with `tc netem` on two Raspberry Pi 5, not Wi-Fi** (netns + GRE tap over the rig's Ethernet; the Pis' real `eth0`/`wlan0` untouched, qdisc and addresses unchanged afterwards; Pis clean). Library: `ab/adaptive-next` ae5c192a (tree `eaa0b2b6`). Branch `ab/wifi-adaptive-s1`. 36 runs, 3 per arm and size, 0 lost, 0 aborts. Raw data `~/rig_results_safe/wtc_ratestep_20261122`, per-phase scorer `/tmp/qrs2.py` output in `results/ratestep_tables.txt`. No library change in this commit (harness only: `large_trace` bench build, `wtc_build_trace.sh`, a fixed-kbit arm, `wtc_chainRS.sh`).
**Link:** profile M1 (5 ms each way = 10 ms round trip, 1 % loss, queue 100), rate **30 -> 4 Mbit/s at 45 s -> 30 Mbit/s at 90 s** (steps applied on both taps with `tc qdisc change`, logged to `<run>.steps`), 135 s runs, 0.25 Hz (34 samples; 12 / 11 / 11 per phase), one 1 MiB or 128 KiB RELIABLE KEEP_LAST 10 flow. The gradual ramp was not run (budget).
**Arms:** **A** `tickle-ad` (ADAPTIVE, no prior); **B** `tickle-ad-c30` (ADAPTIVE with a 30 Mbit/s prior, wrong after the step down); **fixed30** `tickle-p95` (`pacing_rate` 28.2 Mbit/s = 95 % of 30, wrong after the step); **fixed4** `tickle-k3770` (`pacing_rate` 3.77 Mbit/s = 95 % of 4, wrong in the 30 phases); Fast DDS 2.14.6 and CycloneDDS 0.10.5 (publisher lines print `scored=0` by design; the subscriber scores 33 of 33).
**Phase attribution:** by the sample's publish time (index x 4 s) against the step times; the clock offset is subtracted as in the earlier stages. A sample published just before a step (index 11 at 44 s, index 22 at 88 s) is in flight when the rate changes, so it lands in the earlier phase with the later link's latency: the phase max (and p99, which with 33-36 samples is the max) carries that boundary effect for every arm. I therefore give p50 / p90 / max. For the DDS stacks the per-sample file has latency only (delivery order); all 33 samples were delivered in every DDS run, so the order maps to indexes.

## 3. Answers
1. **Does ADAPTIVE (no prior) beat fixed 95 % of 30 when the link changes? Yes, at 1 MiB, clearly, in the slow phase:** p50 2,267 against 5,307 ms (2.3x faster), retransmissions 1,815 against 28,873 per run (16x fewer), datagrams 29 k against 54 k; the fast phases are as good (331 / 348 against 324 / 323 ms, within 2-8 %). A fixed rate set for the fast link floods the slow link in the 4 Mbit/s phase, as the Stage A/B unpaced result predicted. At 128 KiB the fixed 30 Mbit/s and the prior-30 arm B are as good as or better than A in every phase (the 128 KiB sample is smaller than the queue), and A is the slowest ADAPTIVE case in the phase after the step up (93 against 44 ms, 5 samples to recover).
2. **Does it come close to the fixed 95 % of the right rate in each phase? At 1 MiB: yes in all three** (phase 1 331 against 324 ms; phase 2 2,267 against 2,379 ms (fixed4, the right rate for that phase; ADAPTIVE is 5 % faster); phase 3 348 against 323 ms). No single fixed rate does that: fixed30 loses phase 2 by 2.3x, fixed4 loses phases 1 and 3 by 7x. At 128 KiB: phases 1 and 2 close (50 against 44 ms; 281 against 279 ms), phase 3 not (93 against 44 ms for 20 s).
3. **Prior (arm B) does not help when it becomes wrong:** at 1 MiB it delivered 27 of 33 in the slow phase (p50 8.8 s, p90 20 s, 7 samples to recover, retransmissions 7,840) and is worse than A and than fixed30 there; it is equal to the fixed rate while the prior is right. Consistent with quick tests 9-11.
4. **The DDS stacks queue the backlog and do not recover within the run:** after the step up Cyclone's and Fast DDS's latencies are 2.5 s and 25 s (1 MiB) and 313 and 618 ms (128 KiB), while all samples are delivered eventually (33 of 33). ADAPTIVE A is better than both after the step up (348 ms) and than Fast DDS in the slow phase; CycloneDDS is faster than ADAPTIVE in the slow phase (820 ms against 2,267 ms at 1 MiB in the 4 Mbit/s phase; this is as measured and I did not investigate why a CycloneDDS 1 MiB sample arrives in 0.8 s over a 4 Mbit/s link: its samples may not all be sent at the new rate, or the phase attribution by publish time does not hold for it; treat that cell as unexplained).
5. **Caveats:** one flow, one loss pattern (1 % independent), one step pair, 3 runs per arm and size (a phase has 11-12 samples per run); the rate swing of A is large (median 88x, max 144x at 1 MiB; 15x / 21x at 128 KiB) and the first sample of a phase can see the old rate (max column); the fixed-rate emulation says nothing about a real Wi-Fi link's rate adaptation, loss and contention.

## 4. Recommendation: (b) ADAPTIVE as an opt-in experimental option, without a prior, with fixed pacing remaining the documented choice for a known static link
Evidence: when the link rate changes, ADAPTIVE (no prior) follows it at 1 MiB (within 8 % of the best fixed rate in each phase; 16x fewer retransmissions and 2.3x lower p50 than the fixed rate set for the fast link in the slow phase), which no fixed rate can do, and it recovers after both steps within the first sample. It is no better than a fixed rate on a static link (Stage C, v8: it trails the fixed 95 % arm in tails) and worse at 128 KiB after a step up (20 s to recover, p50 2x). A wrong or stale prior (arm B / arm C earlier) hurts, so the documented mode should be "no prior"; the rate swing and the tails (a boundary sample 6-9 s in the fast phase) should be documented as limits. A decision for 2.2.0 belongs to the user: (a) fixed only keeps 2.2.0 simplest and matches the earlier evidence; (b) is justified by this stage for links that change, but rests on one emulated link and three runs per arm. Not changed: no library, wire, API, default, merge or tag in this stage.
